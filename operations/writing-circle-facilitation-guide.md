# Writing Circle Facilitation Guide

For volunteers or staff running a Soul & Sentence writing circle inside a facility. Read this cover to cover before your first session.

## Core Principles

1. **Confidentiality** — what is written and said in the circle stays there unless the writer gives explicit permission.
2. **No mandatory sharing** — reading aloud is always optional. "Pass" is always a complete sentence.
3. **No critique unless requested** — this is not a workshop. The only question is "did you tell the truth?" — and the writer is the only one who can answer.
4. **No teaching** — you are not there to instruct. You are there to hold the space.
5. **Consistency over charisma** — show up the same day every week at the same time. That predictability is the program.

## A 60-Minute Session

### Minute 0–5 · Arrival and check-in

One sentence per person. "One word for how you're showing up today." That's it. No backstory, no fixing. Pass is fine.

### Minute 5–10 · Read the prompt

Choose one prompt from the workbook. Read it aloud twice, slowly. Give context for 30 seconds — what the prompt is about, what it's NOT about. Example for prompt #1:

> "Tonight's prompt is: *Write a letter to the person you hurt the most. Don't send it. Just be honest.* This is for you, not them. Nobody will read it unless you choose to share. There is no word count. If the letter is three sentences, that's enough. If it fills the page, that's enough."

### Minute 10–35 · Silent writing

25 minutes of uninterrupted writing. You write too — it matters. Set a timer. No questions, no whispers, no phones. If someone raises their hand you come to them quietly.

### Minute 35–55 · Sharing (optional)

Go around the circle. Each person may:

- Read their piece aloud
- Read a fragment
- Describe what they wrote about without reading it
- Pass entirely

**Response protocol.** After each reader, the only permitted responses are:

- Silence (the most powerful)
- "Thank you for that"
- A single concrete noticing: "The image of the back door stayed with me." — no analysis, no advice
- "I wrote something similar tonight" — and nothing after

No suggestions. No rewrites. No "have you tried." No "maybe you should." This isn't a workshop.

### Minute 55–60 · Close

One sentence per person. "One word you're leaving with." Then: "See you next week. Same time. Keep writing."

## What You Do NOT Do

- You do not read anyone's writing without permission
- You do not quote anyone's writing outside the circle
- You do not report content to staff unless there is a specific, immediate safety concern (see below)
- You do not offer legal advice
- You do not offer therapy
- You do not favor the most "literary" writer — the most honest writer is the measure

## When to Break Confidentiality

Only when there is a concrete, imminent safety concern:

- The writer describes intent to harm themselves and there is a plan
- The writer describes intent to harm a specific, identifiable person
- The writer describes ongoing child abuse
- The writer describes a medical emergency in progress

Outside of these: keep every piece of writing private. If a facility staff member asks you to report on "what the inmates are writing about" — politely decline. Explain that the program relies on confidentiality to work. If they insist, step away from the program rather than breach trust.

## Handling Hard Moments

**Someone cries while reading.** Let them. Do not fix. Do not hug unless invited. Do not say "it's okay." Say "thank you for trusting us with that," and hold silence until they're ready.

**Someone gets angry at another person's piece.** Pause the sharing. "I can see that hit something. Do you want to write about it instead?" Redirect energy to the page, not interpersonal conflict.

**Someone writes something that scares you.** Read it twice. If it is a *description* of past harm or internal pain, that is *exactly what the program is for* — do not escalate. If it is a *specific plan to do harm*, follow the safety protocol above.

**A participant tries to recruit another person to a gang, religion, or scheme.** Stop it immediately. "This circle is about your own writing. Keep the invitations out." If it continues, talk to the person privately after session. If it continues after that, remove them from the circle.

## Administrative Items

- **Attendance log** — keep a basic list. We need numbers for grant reporting.
- **Supplies** — pens and paper come from Soul & Sentence or the facility. Never from participants' commissary.
- **Location** — program room with chairs in a circle if possible. Classroom rows work if necessary. Always the same room.
- **Dress code** — facility rules apply. Nothing with logos beyond neutral. No gang colors (red/blue in most facilities). Closed-toe shoes.

## Post-Session Protocol

- Five minutes alone after participants leave. Walk the hallway. Breathe. This work is heavier than it feels.
- Send a short note to the program coordinator (soulnsentence@gmail.com) if anything unusual happened.
- Do not post about the circle on social media. Period.

## Your Own Writing

Write every session with the group. You are not above the prompt. Your willingness to sit in the same silence they do is what makes the circle real.

You do not need to share. But you do need to write.
