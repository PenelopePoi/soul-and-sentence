Repentance by the Pen — Family & Growth Workbook

Writing for accountability, connection, learning, and a future beyond incarceration. Inspired by Alex’s advocacy for her brother. Printable pages for individuals and families.

Keep family connections alive

Start with care, not demands: “I’ve been thinking about you. One good memory I have is ____. This week I learned ____. If you want to write back, I would love to hear about ____.” Let children set their own pace. Do not ask them to carry adult worries.

For children: share a drawing prompt, a short story you can both continue, three things you appreciate, or a question about their day. Follow caregiver preferences and facility mail rules.

Making amends without asking for forgiveness

“I take responsibility for ____. It affected you by ____. I understand an apology does not undo it. The change I am working on is ____. You do not owe me a reply or forgiveness.”

Never send a letter if contact is prohibited or unwanted. An unsent letter can still be a useful accountability exercise. Discuss sensitive case facts with counsel before writing or mailing them; ordinary correspondence may be monitored.

Journal prompts

  1. What happened, without excuses or blaming another person?
  2. What feeling did I struggle to name?
  3. What could I do differently next time?
  4. What is one promise I can keep this week?
  5. Which relationship needs patience rather than a response?
  6. What would a stable first week after release require?

GED practice — try first, then check

Open the 12-question practice with feedback

1. Solve 3x + 5 = 20. 2. A $40 item is reduced by 25%. What is its new price? 3. Read: “Jordan studied for 20 minutes daily and tracked missed questions.” Which detail shows a repeatable study habit? 4. In an experiment, why keep all conditions except the tested variable the same?

Answers and explanations

1. x = 5: subtract 5, then divide by 3. 2. $30: 25% of $40 is $10. 3. Studying for 20 minutes daily establishes a routine. 4. It helps isolate the effect of the tested variable. These are original practice exercises, not official GED questions or a readiness prediction.

Legal education and case research

Start with the U.S. Constitution, Florida Courts Help, and the Florida Supreme Court opinions. Record the court, date, citation, issue, holding, later treatment, and why a case may or may not apply. A similar story alone does not make a case controlling.

Ask your public defender: What are the charges and elements? What discovery is missing? What deadlines or hearings are next? What options should we discuss? Pro se representation carries procedural responsibilities; educational templates do not replace advice about your own case.

Resource calls — verify before relying on a number

Jail calling rules vary. No number on these pages has been verified as callable from your facility. Ask staff or your family to confirm the organization’s current official number, acceptance of correctional calls, approved-list requirements, fees, hours, and verification date. Use the call log below to record the result rather than assuming access.

Reentry: one step at a time

Plan identification, a safe place to stay, transportation, prescribed care, benefits, education, income, and a reliable support contact. Write who will help with each step and what can be arranged before release. These pages supplement the existing 33-section workbook.
