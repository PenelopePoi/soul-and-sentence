# Section 1: Constitutional Rights — Know Your Rights

The United States Constitution is the supreme law of the land. Even behind bars, you are protected by it. Understanding your rights is not defiance — it is citizenship.

## The Bill of Rights — Your First 10 Amendments

### 1st Amendment — Freedom of Speech, Religion, Press, Assembly, Petition

You retain the right to practice your religion, to write and send mail, to petition the government for grievances, and to express your views. Prisons may impose "reasonable restrictions" for security purposes, but they cannot eliminate them entirely.

**Key Case:** Turner v. Safley (1987) — Prison regulations restricting inmates' constitutional rights are valid only if "reasonably related to legitimate penological interests."

### 4th Amendment — Protection Against Unreasonable Search & Seizure

While inmates have reduced 4th Amendment protections, the government cannot conduct searches purely for harassment or humiliation. Body cavity searches require reasonable suspicion.

**Key Case:** Hudson v. Palmer (1984) — Inmates do not have a reasonable expectation of privacy in their cells, but the 4th Amendment may still apply to body searches.

### 5th Amendment — Right Against Self-Incrimination, Due Process

You cannot be forced to testify against yourself. You have the right to due process before the government can deprive you of life, liberty, or property.

### 6th Amendment — Right to Counsel, Speedy Trial, Jury

You have the right to an attorney. If you cannot afford one, the state must provide a public defender. You have the right to a speedy and public trial by an impartial jury.

### 8th Amendment — Prohibition of Cruel & Unusual Punishment

This includes the right to adequate medical care, protection from violence, and basic human conditions of confinement.

**Key Case:** Estelle v. Gamble (1976) — "Deliberate indifference" to serious medical needs violates the 8th Amendment.

**Key Case:** Farmer v. Brennan (1994) — Prison officials who show deliberate indifference to a substantial risk of serious harm violate the 8th Amendment.

### 14th Amendment — Equal Protection & Due Process

No state shall deny any person equal protection of the laws. Prisons cannot discriminate based on race, religion, or national origin.

**Key Case:** Wolff v. McDonnell (1974) — Minimum due process requirements for prison disciplinary proceedings.
