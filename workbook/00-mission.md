# Soul & Sentence: Redemption by the Pen

## Our Mission

Soul and Sentence is a creative writing program for incarcerated individuals. We believe that writing is the most honest form of self-examination. We believe that every person, regardless of their charge, their past, or their sentence, has a story worth telling and a truth worth finding.

This is not therapy. This is not school. This is not a program that promises early release or parole board points. This is something more dangerous than all of that: it is an invitation to be honest with yourself.

> "The pen does not judge. It does not sentence. It does not parole. It simply holds what you give it. And if you give it the truth, it gives you back something no court can grant: self-knowledge."

## How It Works

**Step 1: Receive Your Prompts** — Each participant receives writing prompts designed to guide you through layers of self-reflection. You work through them at your own pace.

**Step 2: Write Without Filter** — No word count. No format requirement. No grade. The only rule is honesty.

**Step 3: Reflect and Revisit** — After you write, let it sit. Come back to it the next day. The reflection is as important as the writing.

**Step 4: Submit (If You Choose)** — Participants may submit their writing for the Soul and Sentence anthology. Submission is completely voluntary.

**Step 5: Community** — Where available, Soul and Sentence facilitates writing circles. No one is required to read aloud or share.

## Writing Prompts

1. "Write a letter to the person you hurt the most. Don't send it. Just be honest."
2. "Describe the moment everything changed. Not the arrest — the moment before."
3. "What would you say to your younger self if you could go back?"
4. "Write about someone who believed in you when you didn't deserve it."
5. "What does freedom actually mean to you — not the place, the feeling?"
6. "Describe the worst version of yourself. Now describe who you're becoming."
7. "Write about a memory that still keeps you up at night."
8. "If your sentence ended tomorrow, what would you do first? Be specific."
9. "Write a letter to the judge, the jury, or the system. Say what you couldn't say."
10. "Describe the person you want your child (or family) to see when they visit."
11. "What truth have you been avoiding? Write it down. Nobody will read this but you."
12. "Write your own eulogy — the version where you lived the life you wanted."

## Reflection Exercises

### Exercise 1: Shadow Inventory

Write three columns:
- **Column 1:** The pattern (what you kept doing)
- **Column 2:** The trigger (what set it off each time)
- **Column 3:** The exit (what you could have done differently)

### Exercise 2: Accountability Without Shame

Write down the things you are responsible for. Then write: "I am also [this]." Both lists are true at the same time.

### Exercise 3: Future Self Letter

Write FROM who you want to become TO who you are now. Start: "I'm writing to you from the other side of this. Here's what I need you to know..."
