# Section 7: Pro Se Filing — Representing Yourself in Court

"Pro se" means representing yourself without an attorney. Bounds v. Smith (1977) established that prisons must provide adequate law libraries or legal assistance.

## Types of Pro Se Filings

- **Habeas Corpus (28 U.S.C. 2254)** — Challenges legality of conviction or sentence
- **Section 1983 Complaint** — Challenges constitutional rights violations
- **Motion for Post-Conviction Relief (FL Rule 3.850)** — New evidence, ineffective counsel
- **Motion to Correct Illegal Sentence (FL Rule 3.800)** — Sentence exceeds legal maximum
- **Grievance Appeals** — Appeals of denied grievances
- **Mandamus Petitions** — Orders officials to perform legal duties

## 10 Rules for Effective Filing

1. Be clear and concise
2. State facts, not emotions
3. Cite the law
4. Follow the format
5. Keep copies of everything
6. Meet deadlines (Section 2254: 1 year; Rule 3.850: 2 years)
7. Exhaust administrative remedies first
8. Be truthful — lying is perjury
9. Request IFP status if needed
10. Be patient and persistent

## Sample Complaint Structure

I. Caption — Names, court, case number
II. Jurisdiction — Why this court has authority
III. Statement of Facts — Chronological, with dates and names
IV. Legal Claims — Which rights were violated and how
V. Relief Requested — What you want the court to do
VI. Verification — Signature under penalty of perjury

## Journal Prompts

1. Is there a legal issue you've wanted to address? Write it out clearly.
2. Practice writing a "Statement of Facts" for any situation.
3. What scares you most about representing yourself?
