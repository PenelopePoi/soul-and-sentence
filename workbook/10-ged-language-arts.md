# Section 10: GED Preparation — Language Arts & Reading Comprehension

## Reading Comprehension
- **Main Idea** — The central point. "If I had to summarize this in one sentence, what would I say?"
- **Supporting Details** — Facts, examples, evidence supporting the main idea
- **Inferences** — Conclusions drawn from evidence and reasoning
- **Author's Purpose** — To inform, to persuade, or to entertain

## Writing Skills
- Complete sentence: subject + predicate ("She ran.")
- Fragment: incomplete ("Running fast.")
- Run-on: two complete thoughts without proper punctuation

## Common Errors
- Their/There/They're
- Its/It's
- Your/You're
- Subject-verb agreement

## The GED Essay Structure
1. Introduction: state your thesis
2. Body 1: first reason with evidence
3. Body 2: second reason with evidence
4. Body 3: third reason or counterargument (optional)
5. Conclusion: restate thesis and summarize

## Journal Prompts
1. Write a paragraph, then check it for errors. Rewrite it correctly.
2. What piece of writing has had the biggest impact on your life?
3. Write a 3-5 paragraph essay: "People who have been to prison understand justice better than people who haven't." Agree or disagree.
